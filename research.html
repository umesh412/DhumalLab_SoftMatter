Research

Soft Matter Across Scales

The Dhumal Lab aims to advance the understanding and design of soft matter systems through coarse-grained modeling, multiscale simulation, and microstructural analysis. Our work seeks to connect molecular-scale interactions with emergent structure, dynamics, phase behavior, transport, and macroscopic material properties.

Research Directions

Current & Future Research

Our emerging research program focuses on four interconnected areas spanning colloids, functional materials, pharmaceutical systems, and biological soft matter.

Pharmaceutical Soft Matter

Emulsions & Drug Precipitation

We aim to develop multiscale models of multiple emulsions as controlled environments for drug nucleation, crystallization, and precipitation. Particular emphasis will be placed on confinement, interfacial effects, surfactant dynamics, and transport within droplets, with the long-term goal of guiding the rational design of pharmaceutical formulations for poorly soluble compounds.

Colloids

Polydisperse Colloids & Concentrated Suspensions

We combine PRISM theory with molecular and Brownian dynamics simulations to investigate how particle-size distributions influence equilibrium structure, aggregation, phase stability, phase transitions, and material response in concentrated suspensions. The goal is to establish predictive structure-property relationships relevant to complex formulations and functional soft materials.

Functional Materials

Polymer Nanocomposites & Anisotropic Particles

We investigate how particle shape, size, concentration, and polymer-particle interactions govern dispersion, self-assembly, and phase behavior in polymer nanocomposites. Particular emphasis will be placed on anisotropic fillers and on connecting nanoscale organization with macroscopic properties such as mechanical response, thermal stability, transport, and conductivity.

Biological Soft Matter

Biological & Therapeutic Soft Matter

We are developing coarse-grained and multiscale frameworks for understanding interactions, organization, and transport in biological macromolecular systems. Future directions include protein-based soft matter such as β-lactoglobulin, with emphasis on connecting molecular interactions to collective structure and macroscopic behavior.

Research Background

Previous Research

Previous work provides the scientific foundation for the current research program, spanning colloidal phase behavior, polymer nanocomposites, biological soft matter, and large-scale molecular and Brownian dynamics simulations.

Phase Separation in Colloidal Dispersions

Theory and molecular dynamics simulations were used to investigate depletion-driven phase separation in mixtures of hard colloids and soft macromolecules. These systems exhibited both macrophase separation and composition-dependent microphase organization, providing insight into how competing entropic interactions generate complex soft-matter structures.

Polymer Nanocomposite Phase Behavior

PRISM theory and molecular dynamics simulations were used to study depletion, bridging, chemical anisotropy, and polymer-mediated assembly in polymer-nanoparticle and polymer-nanorod systems.

Colloidal Phase Transitions

Large-scale Brownian dynamics simulations were used to investigate liquid, crystalline, and coexisting states in concentrated colloidal systems, including spontaneous formation of macroscopic liquid-crystal coexistence.

Coarse-Grained Modeling of Therapeutic Antibodies

Colloidal-scale models of monoclonal antibodies were developed to investigate how domain-specific interactions, molecular orientation, and steric effects influence aggregation and microstructural organization in concentrated suspensions.
